\section{Limitations and Claim Boundary}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Construct validity.}
ERC-8004 registry ownership is not proof of a current verified agent wallet,
operational service, autonomous execution, payment purpose, or fraud.  The
comparison class is unknown, not human or legitimate.  The project therefore
cannot estimate agent prevalence, fraud risk, or a human--agent error gap.

\paragraph{Data integrity and availability.}
The retained Stage~2 data were built with a lossy transaction-hash deduplication
rule, and the corrected event-complete extraction has not yet been released.
Load-bearing CSV, parquet, and result files are ignored by version control.
Without an immutable public dataset, cutoff, query provenance, license, and
hash manifest, third parties cannot reproduce the numerical audit from a clean
checkout.

\paragraph{Selection, dependence, and leakage.}
The cohort contains only registry proxies with observable Base USDC activity
and counterparties surviving an activity filter.  Addresses share edges and
components.  Feature design, cleaning, weighting, and threshold exploration
used the same population, while graph features were computed before random
address folds.  Nominal bootstrap and cross-fold intervals therefore understate
model-selection and graph-dependence uncertainty.

\paragraph{Synthetic external validity.}
Stage~1 is not reconstructable.  Stage~3 motifs were designed around the score,
lack independently sampled benign twins, and originally reused addresses.
They test code sensitivity, not real incidence, causal attack completion,
adversarial robustness, or expected loss reduction.

\paragraph{Observation gaps.}
Public transfers do not reveal controller intent, mandate scope, prompt/tool
provenance, wallet policy, key compromise, customer consent, deception, victim
harm, case disposition, beneficial ownership, off-chain transfers, or order-book
effects.  These gaps cannot be repaired by more elaborate modeling of the same
four transaction features.

\paragraph{Domain transfer.}
Base USDC activity is not banking data.  Banks and payment providers observe
authenticated customers, beneficiaries, devices, rails, payment messages,
claims, sanctions/KYC context, investigations, and interventions.  No result
here establishes transfer to bank fraud controls.  Fraud, AML, sanctions, and
market-abuse surveillance also require separate legal hypotheses, labels,
metrics, and operating processes.

\paragraph{Privacy and governance.}
Publicly visible addresses and transaction metadata can remain personal data
when linkable.  Entity resolution, profiling, and cross-institutional sharing
require purpose limitation, minimization, access control, retention rules,
lawful-basis analysis, and potentially a data-protection impact assessment.
This work is not legal advice and has not undergone specialist privacy,
financial-crime, fairness, or model-risk review.

\paragraph{Current status.}
The repository is a repaired research prototype with an explicit negative
result and future protocol.  It is \textbf{not submission-ready as an empirical
detection paper and not deployment-ready}.  The publication gate remains
closed until the event-complete corpus and execution/outcome labels exist, an
untouched confirmatory evaluation is run, the package reproduces from a clean
checkout, and an independent review finds no blocking construct or provenance
defect.
